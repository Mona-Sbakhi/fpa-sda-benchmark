# Cover letter — PLOS ONE

Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript "Do plant-inspired operators help to train a pneumonia classifier? A controlled
benchmark and component analysis of a hybrid Flower Pollination–Seed Dispersal algorithm" for consideration
as a Research Article in PLOS ONE.

The study re-examines, under a controlled and fully reproducible protocol, a hybrid of two nature-inspired
optimisers that we had ourselves proposed in an unpublished draft for training a pneumonia classifier on chest
radiographs. With equal evaluation budgets, 50 paired runs, patient-grouped validation and non-parametric
statistics on three endpoints, the hybrid does not outperform its own second component and is below established
optimisers (GA, PSO, L-SHADE) and gradient-trained classifiers. A component ablation shows that a redesigned
variant owes its improvement to a differential-evolution operator and not to the plant-inspired operators.
Fine-tuning the network raises accuracy to 89.0 % on the official test folder and 97.4 % under patient-grouped
five-fold cross-validation, after which all optimisers lie within about one percentage point of each other.

We believe the work fits PLOS ONE because it is a methodologically rigorous negative result with a component
analysis, in an area where single favourable comparisons of new hybrid metaheuristics are common. All code,
configurations and per-run outputs, including test-set predictions, are public
(https://github.com/Mona-Sbakhi/fpa-sda-benchmark; archived at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24129035),
and every table, figure and in-text number is generated from those files by a single script.

The manuscript is original, has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. The study uses
only a public, de-identified dataset. The authors declare no competing interests and received no specific
funding. The use of a generative AI tool is disclosed in the Acknowledgments.
